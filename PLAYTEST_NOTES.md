## 4. Observer checklist and questions

Answer from observed behavior where possible. Mark uncertain conclusions as hypotheses.

### Joining and onboarding

- Could everyone join through the QR without help? If not, where did they get stuck?
- How long did assembling and readying the squad take?
- Did players understand that their phone contained information others could not see?
- Did they discover that they needed to read instructions aloud? What prompted that discovery?
- What did the first encounter teach them? Did the same confusion recur afterward?
- Which labels, controls, or instructions needed explanation?

Notes:

>

### Planning and private intel

- Did players discuss a shared plan or mostly choose silently?
- Did everyone share their clues? Were important clues overlooked, misunderstood, or forgotten?
- Could the squad connect intel to gear and route decisions?
- Were essential gear and route constraints understood before locking in?
- Did somebody dominate planning or decide for everyone else?
- Did players lock in deliberately, feel rushed, or get auto-drafted?
- Was the planning window too long, too short, or about right? What behavior supports that view?
- Did route and gear choices feel meaningful, or was one plan obviously best?

Notes:

>

### Communication and participation

- Did readers feel necessary, or did operators try to solve everything alone?
- Did players speak to specific teammates or talk over each other?
- Could everyone hear and understand the instructions?
- Were any players idle for long stretches? Who, when, and approximately how long?
- Were support/lookout roles engaging and understandable?
- Did anyone resort to showing or passing their phone? Why?
- Did roles and attention feel reasonably distributed across the session?
- Was there a memorable moment of coordination, relief, laughter, or tension?

Notes:

>

### Controls and readability

- Could TV text and event assignments be read from the seating positions?
- Could phone instructions be understood at a glance under time pressure?
- Were buttons easy to find and tap? Were there accidental taps or excessive scrolling?
- Did radio/sniper adjustments feel responsive and precise enough?
- Did players understand how synchronized holds worked, including releasing and retrying?
- Was color information understandable to everyone, with text labels helping where needed?
- Did players know whether they were the operator, reader, verifier, lookout, or support?
- Did sound help, distract, or go unnoticed? Was it enabled?

Notes:

>

### Difficulty, feedback, and fairness

- For each notable failure, was the likely cause communication, unclear UI, forgotten intel, timing, a technical interruption, or something else?
- Did the squad know immediately whether an encounter succeeded or failed?
- Could they explain why it failed without your help?
- Were the action windows long enough once players understood the task?
- Did incorrect answers and rejected commands produce useful feedback?
- Did alert and wounds change behavior? Were their consequences understood?
- Were flare/medkit opportunities noticed and used deliberately?
- Did an early failure create tension, or make the rest feel hopeless?
- Were failures recoverable and wins earned, rather than arbitrary?

Notes:

>

### Pacing and campaign

- Where did attention drop? Where did tension peak?
- Were there stretches of waiting without anything useful to do?
- Was there enough breathing room between encounters?
- Was the transition between missions clear?
- Did players notice or understand the first mission's effect on the second?
- Did the final objective feel like a climax?
- Did the ending, score, and MVP recognition match the squad's experience?
- Did the session feel too short, too long, or about right? Why?

Notes:

>

### Technical reliability

- Did any phone dim, sleep, lock, or lose its wake-lock?
- Did anyone switch apps or background their controller? What happened on return?
- Were there disconnects, reloads, delays, stuck controls, or confusing rejoin behavior?
- Did any holds remain active unexpectedly or reset unexpectedly?
- Did a technical problem cause an encounter failure? Which one?
- Were there layout problems on a particular phone/browser?
- Could the host save notes and download reports successfully?

For a bug, include the device/browser, mission/encounter, expected behavior, actual behavior, and reproduction steps if known. Capture a screenshot when useful, with participants' consent.

## 5. Questions for each player immediately afterward

Ask players individually before a group discussion if practical, so one confident answer does not set the tone. Ask for concrete examples before suggesting explanations or changes.

Copy this block for each participant. Codenames are sufficient; real names are unnecessary.

### Player: __________

- Which gear/roles did you have?
- **When did you feel essential to the team?**
- **When were you confused, frustrated, or waiting?**
- **Did you understand why encounters succeeded or failed?** Give an example.
- What did you think you were supposed to do in the first encounter?
- Was there something you understood only after failing once?
- Did you have enough time to read, communicate, and act?
- Did any control feel awkward or unresponsive?
- Did your route and gear choices feel meaningful?
- What was your favorite moment? What was your least favorite?
- Did any failure feel unfair? What made it feel that way?
- **Would you want to play again? Why or why not?**
- **What would you change first?**
- Anything else you wanted to say but were not asked?

### Interpretation

- Which encounters had unusually slow first actions or repeated submissions?
- Do those timings match what you observed?
- Did timeouts reflect insufficient time, missed instructions, inactivity, or technical trouble?
- Were rejected commands caused by unclear prerequisites, stale actions, or something else?
- Were there failures that the squad could not explain even though the report identifies the server-side cause?
- Did one mission, role, or player repeatedly struggle? What context might explain it?
- What important parts of the experience were invisible to the metrics?

**Do not infer understanding, enjoyment, or spoken communication from timestamps alone.** Wrong-answer and rejected-command counts can overlap; do not add them as independent mistakes. Drill response times are not directly comparable with normal-timer runs. One small session is evidence, not proof of balance.
